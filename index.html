      How to play

      Hexadek is a puzzle about sorting coloured tiles. Each cell of the board holds a stack of hex tiles. Your goal is to build stacks of 12 tiles all of one colour, called a hexadek. A finished hexadek clears from the board and scores points.

      Stacks

      The tiles on top that share a colour are the stack's top run. The symbol on top shows its colour, and the number shows how many tiles are in it. This stack's top run is 3 blue tiles.

      To see every tile in a stack, press and hold it (or rest the mouse on it): a card lists its colours from the top down. Letting go closes it without moving anything.

      Pouring

      →
      • Tap a stack, then tap a neighbouring cell, or drag the stack there.
      • Its whole top run moves: onto a stack with the same top colour, or onto an empty cell.
      • Only neighbours: the six cells touching it.
      • A stack holds at most 12 tiles. If the whole run doesn't fit, only what fits moves.
      • Journey: 72 levels, each a fixed deck with its own rules. See Journey: levels and stars below.
      • Classic: play until the board fills, for a best score. Each pour uses one: you start with 3, get 3 more for each stack you place and 1 for each hexadek, and can save up to 9. Undo gives the pour back, and you have 5 undos per game.
      • Custom: choose the colours (3 to 13; fewer is easier), limited or free pours (free pours come with unlimited undos), and the length: until the board is full, or a deck of 30 or 50 stacks to place without running out of room. The bar below the board shows how far through a deck you are.
      • Choose under New game.

      Placing

      • Below the board are three new stacks to choose from, and a preview of the next one.
      • Drag one onto an empty cell, or tap it and then tap the cell. Placing ends your turn.

      Clearing and scoring

      12 tiles of one colour clear: 120 points.

      Combos

      A turn lasts from one placement to the next, and you can pour as often as your pours allow. Each hexadek you make in the same turn is worth more: the first 120, the second 240 (×2), the third 360 (×3), and so on. The status line shows the multiplier.

      • So build two or three stacks up to nearly 12, then finish them one after another before you place.
      • Combos are the way to ★★★ on most levels.

      The end

      Each turn adds a stack, so the board fills up. The game ends when there's no empty cell and no pour can make one.

      Journey: levels and stars

      Journey is the main game: 72 levels in 6 chapters, each bringing something new. Each level is a fixed deck of stacks, the same every time you play it. Place every stack to finish it, and the next level opens. Under New game, Journey › Continue goes to your next level, and All levels shows them all with your stars. The first level of each chapter is always open.

      While you play a level, the line under the title shows where you are in its chapter, such as Chapter 2 · 5 of 12, with a dot for each of the chapter's levels: gold once it has stars, ringed for the one you're playing. Tap it, or the level name, to see the level's card and your Journey so far.

      Finishing earns stars, from one to three:

      • ★ Finish the deck.
      • ★★ Finish, and reach the level's score target.
      • ★★★ Finish with a higher score: it takes nearly every hexadek the deck allows, plus a few double or triple clears in one turn. On levels with stones, ★★★ also needs every stone broken, with a slightly lower score.

      Each level's goals show at the start of the game, and the end screen ticks off the ones you met. Your best result for each level is kept, and shown in the level list. A worse game never takes stars away.

      Stones

      →
      • Stones are grey tiles with a crack. Some levels start with them on the board.
      • A stone can't be poured, and nothing can be poured onto it.
      • When a stack clears, each stack next to it loses its top stone, and whatever was under it is free again. Above, the full red stack clears and breaks the stone beside it, uncovering the yellow tile.
      • Some cells hold two stones, one on top of the other, and need two clears next to them.
      • Breaking stones earns no points, but it frees up cells, and on a stones level it's the way to ★★★.

      Tips

      • Pick one or two colours to build up, and give them room: keep their neighbours free for pouring.
      • Use an empty cell to park a top run and uncover the colour underneath.
      • Try not to bury a colour under a different one.
      • Keep two or three cells empty when you can, and check the next stack before you place.
      • When pours are limited, spend them where they finish or set up a hexadek, and save the rest for a crowded board.
      • Undo is free until you place, so experiment.

      Controls

      • Tap or click, or drag. Tapping during an animation skips it. If your phone is set to reduce motion, the game skips its animations.
      • Press and hold a stack, or rest the mouse on it, to see all its tiles.
      • Undo is below the board. Esc lets go of a selected stack, and Ctrl+Z or Cmd+Z undoes a pour.
      • Haptics, also below the board, makes the phone vibrate on pours and hexadeks. It's off to start with.
      • Restart plays the same game again from the start, with the same stacks, to try another plan. New game deals new stacks.
      • As an app: on Android, open the browser menu and choose Install app or Add to Home screen; on an iPhone, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. It opens full screen and plays offline. Your progress stays with the browser you install it from.

      Your play data

      The game keeps a short log of your finished games on this device (score, stars, hexadeks), for testing and tuning. Copy it to share it.
